A short history

From ancient kingdoms to a young, resilient nation.

Ancient era
Bengal was home to kingdoms such as Pundravardhana and Gauda, and later the Buddhist Pala Empire, which built great monasteries like Paharpur.
1204–1757
Muslim sultanates and the Mughals ruled Bengal, one of the richest regions in the world. Dhaka became a grand Mughal provincial capital.
1757–1947
British colonial rule began after the Battle of Plassey. The Bengal famine of 1943 and Partition in 1947 reshaped the region.
1947–1971
As East Pakistan, Bengalis faced political and cultural neglect. The Language Movement of 21 February 1952 became a symbol of identity, and is now International Mother Language Day.
1971
Led by Sheikh Mujibur Rahman’s call for rights, the Liberation War began in March 1971 and ended with victory on 16 December 1971.
Today
Bangladesh is a fast-growing economy, a global leader in ready-made garments, and known for microfinance and community resilience to climate challenges.

Emergency numbers: 999 (police, fire, ambulance) · 109 (women and children helpline) · 16263 (health helpline). Always confirm numbers locally as they can change.
Everyday safety
  • Keep your phone and wallet in front pockets in crowded places.
  • Use ride apps (Uber, Pathao) in Dhaka for fixed prices, or agree a fare before you start.
  • Avoid walking alone late at night in unfamiliar areas.
  • Keep copies of your passport and visa, and register your SIM with your passport.
Food and water
  • Drink only sealed bottled or filtered water.
  • Choose busy, freshly cooked street food stalls.
  • Carry oral rehydration salts and basic medicines.
  • Be careful with raw salads and ice in small places.
Traffic and roads
  • Traffic is intense. Cross roads slowly and steadily, and walk with locals.
  • Intercity buses can be risky at night. Prefer reputed coach services or trains.
  • Allow extra time as jams are common in Dhaka.
Weather and nature
  • The monsoon (June–September) brings heavy rain and floods.
  • Cyclone season peaks in April–May and October–November. Follow local warnings, especially on the coast.
  • For the Sundarbans and hill areas, go with licensed guides.
Scams and common awareness
  • Ask prices first in markets, and bargain politely.
  • Be wary of overly friendly strangers offering tours or exchange deals. Use banks or licensed exchange counters.
  • Avoid political rallies and protests, as they can turn unpredictable.
  • Check your government’s latest travel advisory before you go.

Culture and etiquette

Small gestures that earn big smiles.

Greetings

Say “Assalamu alaikum” to Muslims, “Nomoshkar” to Hindus, or a simple “Hello”. Use your right hand to give, receive and eat.

Dress

Dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered. Women may carry a light scarf for mosques and rural areas.

Religious sites

Remove shoes before entering mosques, temples and homes. Ask before photographing people, especially women.

Food

Try hilsa fish, kacchi biryani, bhorta, pithas, panta bhat and sweet mishti doi. Rice and fish are the heart of Bengali meals.

Festivals

Pohela Boishakh (14 April), Eid, Durga Puja, Ekushey February, Victory Day and Independence Day (26 March).

Hospitality

You may be invited for tea or a meal. Accepting a cup of tea is a warm way to make friends.

Practical tips for newcomers

Before you land and while you’re here.

Visa and entry

Most foreigners need a visa. Check the official Bangladesh e-visa or embassy website for current rules and fees before travelling.

Money

Cash is widely used. ATMs are common in cities. Mobile wallets like bKash and Nagad are popular among locals. Carry small notes for rickshaws and tea stalls.

Getting around
  • Rickshaws for short trips, CNG auto-rickshaws for medium trips.
  • Dhaka Metro Rail (MRT Line 6) is fast and avoids traffic.
  • Trains (Bangladesh Railway) are scenic and comfortable between major cities.
  • Launches (river ferries) are a classic way to reach the south, such as Dhaka to Barisal.
Internet, SIM and power

Grameenphone, Robi and Banglalink offer SIMs with registration. Power is 220V, with plug types C, D, G and K, and short outages can happen.

Useful Bangla words
  • Dhonnobad: Thank you
  • Kemon achhen?: How are you?
  • Koto taka?: How much?
  • Ami bujhi na: I don’t understand
  • Sahajjo korben?: Can you help me?
